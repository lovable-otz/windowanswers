The complete window replacement guide

Reviewed 2026-08-18Every figure cited to a named source

This is the whole window replacement decision in the order you meet it: working out whether you need replacement at all, measuring, choosing what goes in, getting quotes you can compare, and knowing what a finished job should look like. Each stage links to the detailed answers underneath it.

Most people do not replace windows twice. That means the whole thing is unfamiliar exactly once, while you are being quoted by people who do it every day. This page is the map: the stages in the order they happen, and what the decision at each stage actually is.

The stages, in order

  1. Decide whether replacement is the job. Some symptoms point to replacement, others to repair or to something that isn't the window at all. Start in Common Problems if something is wrong rather than merely old.
  2. Measure and count. How many openings, what sizes, and whether they are standard or custom. This sets the shape of every quote you will receive.
  3. Choose what goes in. Frame material, glazing, and style, and the trade-offs between them. That is Types & Options.
  4. Decide who does it. Whether any of it is realistic to do yourself is DIY vs Professional; how to pick and vet a company is Choosing a Company.
  5. Get quotes you can compare. Same scope, same install method, same glazing, in writing. Costs and what moves them are in What It Costs.
  6. Installation and inspection. What happens on the day, and what to check before the crew leaves and before the final payment.

Two words worth learning before you get quoted

Insert replacement (also called a pocket or retrofit replacement) fits a new window inside your existing frame, which stays in place. Full-frame replacement removes the old frame entirely, back to the rough opening.

They are different jobs, they cost different amounts, and they are not always interchangeable for a given opening. If you learn nothing else before your first sales appointment, learn to ask which one is being quoted. Two quotes that differ on this are not competing quotes.

What this section covers

The pages underneath this one are the general questions about the process: measuring, sizing, what "replacement window" means for different house types, and the brands people ask about by name. Questions about price sit under What It Costs, and questions about a specific material or style sit under Types & Options.

Common questions

Where should I start if I have not done anything yet?

With the stage list above. If nothing is actually broken, stage 2 is the first thing that produces useful information, because the opening count and sizes determine what every quote will look like.

Do I have to replace every window at once?

That is a scope decision, not a technical one, and it is a legitimate thing to ask an installer to quote both ways. Phasing changes the per-window economics, which is covered in the cost section.
